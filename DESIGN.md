# SJH DEV — food ingredients identity

## Brief and direction
The site now presents only the client-approved food ingredients business. Keep the company name, contact details, 28 food products, inquiry-first scope, self-hosted type and deployment workflow. Replace all technical-business content and its visual cues with a food product catalogue. This is a food-focused reworking of the incumbent site, not an unrelated business expansion.

## Subject-led idea
The ingredient sample dish becomes the recurring visual device: the large opening product photograph, square category/product windows, and a shallow bowl within the logo. The ingredients themselves carry color and texture. The site does not suggest an owned farm, manufacturer, certification or consumer grocery checkout.

## Palette and typography
White #ffffff is the principal ground; pale green-gray #f0f2ec separates the sampling and inquiry sections. Dark foliage green #213d2d carries text, utility bar and footer; #355a3a marks actions. The #b3813c seed gold is confined to the logo. This palette comes from the pumpkin seeds, nuts and ceramic dishes in the actual product images. Secondary text is #4c604f, and controls use a #7c8e75 boundary. Existing self-hosted Trade Sans (DM Sans) is preserved for continuity and readability; no new font service is added.

## Logo
A golden almond-like kernel and two green ingredient shapes sit over a shallow green bowl. It is a compact food-ingredient emblem, distinct from the old geometric trade mark. The SVG mark, outlined horizontal wordmark, light variants and browser icon are applied consistently. Wordmark paths come from the local licensed typeface, so the standalone logo does not depend on installed fonts. The logo does not imply organic or other certification.

## Composition and behavior
A quiet brand/navigation header and ingredient search lead to a large paprika sample photograph beside the company proposition. Eight photographed categories follow, then four distinct product samples and a practical sourcing brief. The complete catalogue contains exactly 28 products and eight filters. The inquiry form contains food-related topics and product-specific fields only. Unknown former product parameters cannot populate the form with a withdrawn product.

The site uses flat controls, square product windows, generous section spacing, visible focus states and restrained hover. Desktop category grids adapt to two columns on smaller screens; catalogue and inquiry layouts stack on phones. Native details navigation and a native GET search form preserve simple behavior. The 28 product images remain local and compressed. No repeated homepage image is needed: the hero, eight category covers and four selected samples are distinct.

## Warehousing and packing imagery
The food theme is retained while large operational images add depth. A broad warehouse photograph breaks the product grids with a deep-green packing-requirement panel. Two working scenes support the lower home narrative. The company page uses a bulk-bag scene; the sourcing page presents the full six-scene collection. Food-product images remain the catalogue content; operational scenes do not become product records or inventory claims. Natural warehouse light, kraft cartons, stainless packing tables and plain packaging keep these scenes close to the actual product range. Copy explicitly frames the scenes as illustrations of the details to consider in a sourcing brief. Exact prompts and provenance are recorded in docs/food-scenes-2026-11-03.json.

## SEO category extension
Eight static category routes reuse the existing food product cards, colors, images and typography. Simple breadcrumbs and wrapping category links make the routes findable from the home, footer and complete catalogue. The catalogue is server-rendered for its initial content and retains the same search/filter interface. Each category has a distinct title, description, canonical and sharing metadata, plus an item list and breadcrumb schema. This extension changes discoverability rather than the established food visual identity. The withdrawn technical-business routes now return 404/noindex, replacing the earlier unrelated catalogue redirect. No local preview was started.

## Google favicon compatibility — 2026-11-03
Read-only checks against the client-provided GitHub repository found all 24 SEO patch files matching. The published homepage is food-specific, the live sitemap has 15 URLs, and withdrawn base/detail URLs return 404/noindex. The published SVG favicon matches the new food mark exactly. The build declares ICO first, then PNG, with SVG retained. No business content, metadata, remote repository or deployment was changed by this compatibility addition. No immediate search-result update is claimed.
